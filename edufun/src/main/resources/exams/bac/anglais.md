# Méthode
> Reading comprehension, language skills and an essay (about 150–200 words).
1. Read the questions before the text; underline key words.
2. True/False: justify with a quotation. Reference questions: identify what the pronoun refers to.
3. Answer « in your own words » when asked: do not copy the text.
4. Essay: introduction (topic + opinion), two body paragraphs with examples, conclusion; use linking words.
5. Keep 10 minutes to check tenses, agreement and spelling.

# Sujet type 1 — Youth unemployment
Text:
In many African countries, young people represent the majority of the population. Every year, thousands of graduates enter the job market, but there are not enough jobs for all of them. Some decide to create their own businesses: they sell clothes online, develop mobile applications or transform local products. Governments have launched programmes to support young entrepreneurs, but access to funding remains difficult. Experts believe that training in digital skills and agriculture could create millions of jobs.
1. True or false? Justify. a) Young people are a minority. b) Some graduates create their own businesses.
2. What do young entrepreneurs do? Give two examples.
3. What remains difficult for them?
4. Essay (150 words): « What can young Ivorians do to fight unemployment? »
## Corrigé
1. a) False: « young people represent the majority of the population ». b) True: « Some decide to create their own businesses ».
2. They sell clothes online and develop mobile applications (or transform local products).
3. Access to funding remains difficult.
4. Marking guide: introduction with a clear opinion; two developed ideas (training in digital skills and agriculture, entrepreneurship supported by microcredit and mobile money); examples from Côte d'Ivoire; conclusion; correct tenses and linking words.
# Sujet type 2 — Plastic waste in our cities
Text:
Walk along any street in Abidjan after a heavy rain and you will see the same picture: bottles, sachets and plastic bags blocking the gutters. When the water cannot flow, it floods houses and roads. Plastic does not disappear: it can stay in nature for hundreds of years, and when it is burnt, it releases toxic smoke.
Yet some young people see this waste as a resource. In Yopougon, a group of students collects used bottles and turns them into paving stones and school benches. « We used to think that rubbish was useless, » says Awa, one of the founders. « Now we know it can create jobs. » Their small company employs about twenty people, most of whom are women from the neighbourhood.
Recycling alone, however, is not enough. Consumers must reduce the amount of plastic they use, for example by carrying a reusable basket to the market. Local authorities, for their part, should organise regular waste collection and punish illegal dumping.
**A. Reading comprehension**
1. True or false? Justify with a sentence from the text. a) Plastic disappears quickly in nature. b) The students' company employs mainly women.
2. What does the word « it » refer to in « it releases toxic smoke »?
3. Find in the text words or expressions that mean: a) poisonous; b) throwing away waste where it is not allowed.
4. Why do floods happen after heavy rains, according to the text?
5. Answer in your own words: what two solutions does the author propose besides recycling?
**B. Language**
6. Put into reported speech: Awa said, « Now we know it can create jobs. »
7. Turn into the passive voice: « The students collect used bottles. »
8. Complete with the right relative pronoun: « Awa is the young woman … company recycles bottles. »
**C. Writing (about 150 words)**
9. You are a student in Abidjan. Write a formal letter to the mayor of your municipality to complain about the waste in your neighbourhood and suggest solutions. Sign it « Koffi » or « Aya ».
## Corrigé
**A. Compréhension**
1. a) False: « it can stay in nature for hundreds of years ». b) True: « most of whom are women from the neighbourhood ».
2. « it » renvoie à « plastic » (le plastique brûlé dégage une fumée toxique).
3. a) poisonous = « toxic » ; b) throwing away waste where it is not allowed = « illegal dumping ».
4. Because plastic bottles, sachets and bags block the gutters, so the rain water cannot flow away. (On attend une réponse avec « because », reformulée.)
5. Consumers should use less plastic (for example by taking a reusable basket to the market), and the local authorities should collect waste regularly and punish people who dump rubbish illegally.
**B. Langue**
6. Awa said (that) they knew then that it could create jobs. Explication : au discours indirect, « now » devient « then », « we » devient « they », le présent recule au prétérit (know devient knew, can devient could).
7. Used bottles are collected by the students. (Passif au présent : am/is/are + participe passé.)
8. « whose » : Awa is the young woman whose company recycles bottles. (« whose » exprime la possession.)
**C. Expression écrite — modèle et barème**
Modèle de lettre (environ 150 mots) :
Dear Sir or Madam,
I am writing to complain about the piles of rubbish in our neighbourhood. Every week, plastic bags and bottles block the gutters near our school, and when it rains, the streets are flooded. Some residents also burn waste, which produces toxic smoke and makes children ill.
I would like to suggest some solutions. First, the town hall could organise waste collection twice a week and install public bins. Second, people who dump waste illegally should be fined. Finally, you could support young recyclers, like the students of Yopougon who make paving stones from plastic bottles, as this would create jobs.
I am sure that, with your help, our neighbourhood can become clean and safe again. I look forward to hearing from you.
Yours faithfully,
Aya
Barème indicatif (sur 10) : respect de la forme de la lettre formelle (formule d'appel « Dear Sir or Madam » et formule finale « Yours faithfully », car le nom du destinataire n'est pas connu) 2 points ; pertinence et organisation des idées (plainte, solutions, mots de liaison) 4 points ; correction de la langue (temps, modaux, orthographe) 3 points ; respect de la longueur 1 point.

# QCM
? « Although » expresses:
+ contrast
- cause
- addition
! « Although it was raining, they played. »
? « If I had money, I … a car. »
- will buy
+ would buy
- buy
! Conditional type 2: if + past, would + verb.
? « By 2030, I … my studies. »
+ will have finished
- finish
- finished
! Future perfect: an action completed before a future moment.
? Choose the correct closing for a formal letter starting « Dear Sir or Madam »:
- Yours sincerely
+ Yours faithfully
- Love
! « Yours faithfully » when the name of the person is unknown.
? « He avoided … the question. »
+ answering
- to answer
- answer
! « avoid » is followed by the -ing form.
? « The bridge … in 2014. »
- built
+ was built
- has built
! Passive voice in the past: was + past participle.
? Reported speech: She said, « I am tired. » becomes:
+ She said (that) she was tired.
- She said (that) I am tired.
- She said (that) she is being tired.
! After a past reporting verb, the present simple moves back to the past simple and the pronoun changes.
? « This is the student … father is a doctor. »
- who
+ whose
- which
! « whose » expresses possession: the student's father.
? « You speak English, … ? »
- do you
+ don't you
- aren't you
! Question tag: positive sentence with the present simple, so negative tag with « don't ».
? « When I was a child, I … walk five kilometres to school. »
+ used to
- am used to
- use to
! « used to » + base verb describes a past habit that no longer exists.
? « I have lived in Bouaké … 2015. »
- for
+ since
- during

? « The road is wet. It … have rained last night. »
+ must
- can't

! « must have » + past participle expresses a logical deduction about the past.
